\subsection{Prospective six-wording, three-task Flash extension}

The analysis and decision rule were frozen before the first call in \texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md}. There were 3,672 valid decisions, two repeats per item, three action-tied and three answer-tied triggers, with identical check content within each task. The sample was reduced before collection to fit the budget: 65 airline bases, 30 orders per retail-cancel family, 43 per retail-return family and five clean controls per family/task.

Retail cancel & 0/180 & 0/180 & 30 \\
Retail return & 0/258 & 0/258 & 43 \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}

The mixed-effects logistic model used random intercepts for base/order, paraphrase and task. The preregistration says to report the primary criterion as ``not established'' when the pooled model is unidentifiable; the model is quasi-separated (both retail tasks had zero events), so the pooled odds ratio is not reported, and per-task counts are the result. Only 1/3 tasks pointed in the predicted direction. The prespecified cross-task rule was not met because both retail tasks were at floor (0 refusals under either wording); the retail tasks could not show the effect, so generality beyond this airline task is untested.



The model assumes a common wording-type coefficient despite task heterogeneity; only three tasks and six paraphrases inform their variance components. Both retail tasks share one status/tracking invariant, verified on all 1,000 source orders. A deterministic 120-summary sample was LLM-annotated (by Codex; not an independent human audit), with full records in \texttt{reports/\allowbreak power-review.md}. These are model-generated summaries, not direct observations of all internal computation. 

The fitted task-intercept standard deviation was 13.52 log-odds units. The paired airline refusal difference was 71.3 percentage points (95\% cluster-bootstrap CI [64.6,77.4]); both retail tasks had 0 events under either wording, so their differences are not estimable (both at floor). The exact one-sided 95\% upper bounds on the refusal rate, computed on independent orders rather than calls, are 9.5\% (retail cancel, 30 orders) and 6.7\% (retail return, 43 orders). No bad payouts or return approvals were observed (0/1,656 corrupted yes decisions), and clean-control errors were 0/360 (only five bases per clean family/task).

In the presampled qualitative review (LLM-annotated by Codex; not an independent human audit), 5/10 airline action-tied D summaries and 10/10 answer-tied D summaries showed a concrete arithmetic check. All 20 sampled airline C and all 80 retail summaries showed a concrete check. Seven of 120 summaries referred to a benchmark or test; these generated claims do not establish training contamination.
